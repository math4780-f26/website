Final Project: Learning Multiple Linear Regression with Generative AI

Project
Important

This is an individual project. Plan to work on it over 7 to 10 days rather than in one sitting.

Please check D2L for the final due date and submission link. The downloadable CSV files for all data sets are linked below and included in the project package.

This project asks you to do two things at the same time:

  1. demonstrate your understanding of multiple linear regression, and
  2. show that you can use generative AI as a learning tool in a thoughtful, critical, and responsible way.

The goal is not to let AI complete the assignment for you. The goal is to use AI to help you learn something new, verify ideas, identify weaknesses in your understanding, and communicate your own conclusions clearly.

By completing this project, you will practice how to:

  • explore relationships among variables using graphs
  • choose and justify a multiple linear regression model
  • interpret coefficients and model fit in context
  • check assumptions and discuss limitations
  • use AI as a tutor, coach, critic, and study partner
  • verify AI output instead of accepting it automatically

Submit one PDF file to D2L > Assessments > Dropbox.

Your PDF must include the following parts, in this order:

  1. Data Analysis Report
  2. AI Use Record and Conversation Evidence

AI Use Policy

You may use one general purpose text based generative AI tool as a learning support tool for this project. Examples include ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot, Claude, or another instructor approved tool. You remain responsible for all statistical reasoning, all final written explanations, and all final conclusions.

Allowed uses

You may use AI to:

  • ask for plain language explanations of statistical ideas
  • ask AI to quiz you or question you step by step
  • ask for examples that are not based on your dataset
  • ask what assumptions, limitations, or diagnostic checks might matter
  • ask AI to critique your draft reasoning
  • ask AI to help you notice unclear wording or possible mistakes
  • ask AI to suggest what you should verify on your own

Not allowed uses

You may not use AI to:

  • write your final report for you
  • produce final report ready paragraphs that you submit as your own writing
  • interpret your exact regression output and then copy that interpretation
  • decide your final conclusion for this dataset
  • replace your own reasoning with unverified AI claims

A good rule is this:

AI may support your learning process, but it may not replace your thinking.

Important rule about model complexity

To keep the projects comparable, your final model must:

  • use one quantitative response variable listed above
  • include at least three predictors
  • include at least two quantitative predictors
  • keep the model at a level appropriate for introductory statistics

You may use one categorical predictor if it is relevant.

You do not need interaction terms unless you want to explore one and can justify it clearly.

You may use a simple transformation such as \(\log(\text{response})\) only if your graphs and diagnostics suggest it is reasonable. A transformation is optional, not required.

Project Components

Your project has three parts.

Part 1. Data Analysis Report

Format

  • Length: 3 to 5 pages
  • Spacing: single space
  • Font: Times New Roman, 12 pt
  • Margins: one inch on all sides
  • Do not show code in the report

Download report template

Required sections

1. Introduction

Briefly introduce the context of your chosen data set. Define the response variable and the predictors in your final model. State the goal of the analysis and explain why those predictors are reasonable choices.

2. Exploratory Data Analysis

Create and discuss graphs that help you understand the relationships in your data.

At minimum, include:

  • one graph of the response variable
  • scatterplots of the response versus each quantitative predictor in your final model
  • if you use a categorical predictor, a suitable graph such as a side by side boxplot

Describe patterns you notice. For example, discuss direction, strength, unusual points, possible nonlinearity, and whether the relationships appear roughly linear.

3. Model Building

Fit a multiple linear regression model using your chosen response and predictors.

Include:

  • the fitted regression equation
  • the main model output
  • a brief explanation of why this model is a reasonable choice

If you decide to use a transformed response, briefly explain why.

4. Interpretation of Results

Interpret the following in context:

  • the intercept
  • at least two slope coefficients for quantitative predictors
  • if applicable, one categorical predictor coefficient

Your interpretations should make clear that each coefficient is interpreted while holding the other predictors constant.

5. Model Fit, Assumptions, and Limitations

Discuss:

  • what \(R^2\) tells you in this setting
  • whether the model appears useful for explanation or prediction
  • at least two assumptions or diagnostic concerns
  • at least one limitation of the analysis

If you compared an untransformed and transformed response model, briefly explain which one you chose and why.

6. Conclusion

Write a short conclusion for a general audience. Summarize what the analysis suggests and what it does not prove.

Part 2. AI Use Record and Conversation Evidence

Download AI usage template

It should be about 1 to 2 pages, not counting optional screenshots.

Your goal is to show how you used AI, not to paste an unnecessarily long transcript.

For your most important AI interactions, briefly record:

  • what you were trying to learn
  • what you asked AI
  • what AI told you
  • what you accepted, revised, or rejected
  • how you checked or verified the idea

Then include enough conversation evidence for me to understand your actual AI use. You may include:

  • short transcript excerpts,
  • screenshots,
  • copied chat text,
  • or a share link plus a few selected excerpts.

Start this section with the starter prompt you chose.

A long transcript by itself is not enough. This section should highlight the most meaningful parts of your AI use.

Part 3. Verification and Reflection Memo

Download Reflection template

This section should be about 1 page total.

A. Verification and Critique

Briefly describe:

  • one example where AI was incomplete, vague, misleading, or wrong, and
  • one example where AI genuinely helped you learn something useful.

For the weak AI example, explain in a few sentences:

  • what AI said
  • why it was not fully satisfactory
  • how you checked it
  • what you concluded instead

B. Reflection

Write a short reflection of about 150 to 250 words addressing questions such as:

  • Which AI tool did you use?
  • How did AI help you learn multiple linear regression?
  • What did AI do well?
  • What did AI do poorly?
  • What would you do differently next time?

Starter Prompt Menu

You do not have to use one exact prompt. Instead, choose one of the following starting prompts and then continue the conversation in your own way.

TipPrompt A. Concept Coach

I am learning multiple linear regression for a class project using a real data set. Act as a concept coach. Do not write my report. Do not give me final interpretations for my dataset. Instead, teach me step by step in plain language. After each explanation, ask me one question so I have to think before moving on.

TipPrompt B. Socratic Tutor

I am working on a multiple linear regression project. Act as a Socratic tutor. Ask me one question at a time about model setup, predictor choice, coefficient interpretation, model fit, assumptions, and limitations. Only explain more after I answer. Do not write final report text for me.

TipPrompt C. Critic and Checker

I am going to explain my reasoning about a multiple linear regression project. Your job is to critique my reasoning, point out what is unclear or incomplete, and suggest what I should verify. Do not give me final report ready paragraphs, and do not decide my final conclusion for me.

You may revise the prompt after you begin. What matters is that your interaction supports learning rather than outsourcing.

Project Evaluation

Your project will be evaluated using the following categories.

Category Points
Statistical understanding and correctness 30
Quality of graphs, model setup, and interpretation 20
AI use record and transparency of process 20
Verification, critique, and reflection 25
Writing quality, organization, and professionalism 5

Total: 100 points

Final Advice

A weak project uses AI to generate language.

A strong project uses AI to improve understanding.

The strongest projects show that you:

  • asked meaningful questions,
  • made sensible modeling choices,
  • verified important ideas,
  • noticed when AI was not reliable,
  • and wrote the final analysis in your own words.
