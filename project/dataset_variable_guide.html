Data Dictionary for Final Project Data Sets

This guide explains the variables in the five data sets used for the final project.

Use this guide when you are deciding:

  • which data set to choose
  • which variable should be the response variable
  • which variables could be good predictors
  • how to interpret coefficients in context
Note

These are cleaned course copies of the original data. Variable names in this guide match the CSV files provided with the project.

TipMedical Insurance Charges
Variable Type What it means Values or notes
age Quantitative Age of the individual Measured in years
sex Categorical Recorded sex of the individual female, male
bmi Quantitative Body mass index Higher values usually indicate higher body mass relative to height
children Quantitative Number of children or dependents covered by the insurance plan Whole numbers
smoker Categorical Whether the individual is a smoker yes, no
region Categorical Region of the United States where the individual lives northeast, northwest, southeast, southwest
charges Quantitative Individual medical insurance charges Measured in US dollars

Common project idea: Use charges as the response variable and study how age, BMI, smoking, and number of children relate to medical costs.

TipCar Fuel Efficiency
Variable Type What it means Values or notes
mpg Quantitative Fuel efficiency of the car Miles per gallon
horsepower Quantitative Engine power Horsepower
weight Quantitative Weight of the car Pounds
acceleration Quantitative Acceleration performance measure Measured in seconds. Lower values usually mean faster acceleration.
origin Categorical Where the car was made Usa, Europe, Japan
cylinders Quantitative Number of engine cylinders Common values include 4, 6, and 8
displacement Quantitative Engine displacement Cubic inches
model_year Quantitative Model year of the car Recorded here as two digit years from 70 to 82

Common project idea: Use mpg as the response variable and study how horsepower, weight, acceleration, and origin relate to fuel efficiency.

TipDiamond Prices
Variable Type What it means Values or notes
price Quantitative Price of the diamond Measured in US dollars
carat Quantitative Weight of the diamond Carats
depth Quantitative Total depth percentage A common diamond proportion measure
table Quantitative Table width percentage A common diamond proportion measure
cut Categorical Overall cut quality grade Fair, Good, Very Good, Premium, Ideal
color Categorical Diamond color grade D is closer to colorless; letters farther down the scale indicate more color
clarity Categorical Diamond clarity grade Higher clarity means fewer visible internal flaws
x Quantitative Length of the diamond Millimeters
y Quantitative Width of the diamond Millimeters
z Quantitative Depth of the diamond Millimeters

Common project idea: Use price as the response variable and study how carat, cut, and physical dimensions relate to price.

TipHouse Sale Prices
Variable Type What it means Values or notes
sale_price Quantitative Sale price of the house Measured in US dollars
living_area Quantitative Above ground living area Square feet
bedrooms Quantitative Number of bedrooms above ground level Whole numbers
house_age Quantitative Age of the house at the time of sale Computed from year built and year sold. A value of -1 can occur for very new construction.
overall_quality Quantitative Overall material and finish quality Higher values indicate better quality
overall_condition Quantitative Overall condition of the house Higher values indicate better condition
lot_area Quantitative Lot size Square feet
full_bath Quantitative Number of full bathrooms Whole numbers
neighborhood Categorical Neighborhood code in Ames, Iowa 28 neighborhood categories
year_built Quantitative Year the house was built Calendar year
year_sold Quantitative Year the house was sold Calendar year

Common project idea: Use sale_price as the response variable and study how living area, house age, quality, and other house features relate to sale price.

TipStudent Achievement
Variable Type What it means Values or notes
final_score Quantitative Final course grade Scale from 0 to 20
prior_score Quantitative Later prior grade before the final grade Scale from 0 to 20
first_period_score Quantitative Earlier grade from the first grading period Scale from 0 to 20
study_time Quantitative Weekly study time category 1 = less than 2 hours, 2 = 2 to 5 hours, 3 = 5 to 10 hours, 4 = more than 10 hours
absences Quantitative Number of school absences Whole numbers
internet_access Categorical Whether the student has internet access at home Yes, No
failures Quantitative Number of past class failures Values from 0 to 3
age Quantitative Age of the student Years
sex Categorical Recorded sex of the student Female, Male
go_out Quantitative How often the student goes out with friends Scale from 1 to 5, where larger values mean going out more often
health Quantitative Self reported current health status Scale from 1 to 5, where larger values mean better health

Common project idea: Use final_score as the response variable and study how prior performance, study time, absences, and other student characteristics relate to final achievement.

Tips for choosing variables

When you build your final model, try to choose predictors that make sense in context. A good project does not only fit a model. It also explains why the predictors belong in the model.

A few examples:

  • In the insurance data, smoker is likely an important predictor of charges.
  • In the car data, weight and horsepower are natural predictors of mpg.
  • In the diamond data, carat is likely strongly related to price.
  • In the housing data, living_area and overall_quality are natural predictors of sale_price.
  • In the student data, prior_score and study_time are natural predictors of final_score.

You should still examine graphs and think about whether your final model is reasonable.
