Class Activity Work

How to Use This Page

For each activity:

  1. Think about the problem briefly on your own.
  2. Discuss it with your group.
  3. Be ready to explain your group’s reasoning.
  4. Submit the short exit ticket before leaving class.

There is no penalty for an incorrect answer. The goal is to reason, discuss, and participate.

MSSC 5780 students should also discuss the Graduate Challenge.

Activity 1: Read a Regression

Suggested date: Thu, Sep 17
Topic: Simple Linear Regression
Time: 15–20 minutes

Scenario

A professor studies the relationship between weekly study time and exam score.

Let

  • \(Y\) = exam score from 0 to 100,
  • \(X\) = hours studied during the week before the exam.

A simple linear regression gives

\[ \widehat{\text{Score}} = 58.4 + 3.15(\text{Study Hours}). \]

The estimated standard error of the slope is 0.62, and the 95% confidence interval for the slope is

\[ (1.91,\;4.39). \]

For a student who studies 6 hours:

  • fitted mean score: 77.3
  • 95% confidence interval for the mean score: (74.9, 79.7)
  • 95% prediction interval for one new student’s score: (66.2, 88.4)

Discuss

  1. What does the slope 3.15 mean in this context?

  2. What does the intercept 58.4 mean? Is it useful?

  3. A student studies 6 hours. Which number would you use as the predicted score?

  4. If that student asks, “What range of scores is plausible for my score?”, which interval should you use?

  5. Consider the statement:

    “Studying one extra hour causes a student’s score to increase by 3.15 points.”

    What is potentially wrong with this statement?

Group Challenge

Write a three-sentence summary:

  • one sentence about the relationship,
  • one sentence about prediction,
  • one sentence about an important limitation.
WarningMSSC 5780 Graduate Challenge

Discuss:

  1. Why is the prediction interval wider than the confidence interval for the mean response?
  2. What assumptions are needed for the usual \(t\)-based confidence interval for the slope?

Exit Ticket

Complete this sentence:

One thing I would be careful about when interpreting this regression is __________.

Activity 2: Same X, Different Story

Suggested date: Thu, Oct 1
Topic: Multiple Linear Regression
Time: 15–20 minutes

Scenario

Suppose \(Y\) is a student’s final exam score.

Two models are fitted.

Model 1

\[ \widehat{Y} = 61.2 + 4.80(\text{Study Hours}). \]

Model 2

\[ \widehat{Y} = 42.7 + 2.90(\text{Study Hours}) + 7.60(\text{Prior GPA}). \]

The sample correlations are:

Variables Correlation
Study Hours and Final Exam 0.58
Prior GPA and Final Exam 0.71
Study Hours and Prior GPA 0.46

Discuss

  1. How would you interpret the Study Hours coefficient in Model 1?
  2. How would you interpret the Study Hours coefficient in Model 2?
  3. Why might the coefficient change from 4.80 to 2.90 after Prior GPA is added?
  4. Does the smaller coefficient mean that studying became “less important”?

Group Challenge

Compare these two questions:

Question A:
Among all students, how are study hours associated with final exam score?

Question B:
Among students with similar prior GPA, how are study hours associated with final exam score?

Which model is more directly connected to each question?

Then explain in plain language what “holding Prior GPA constant” means.

WarningMSSC 5780 Graduate Challenge

Use the idea of omitted-variable bias or partial regression to explain why the Study Hours coefficient can change.

Then discuss:

Would adding Prior GPA always make the Study Hours coefficient smaller?

Why or why not?

Exit Ticket

In one sentence:

What is the main difference between interpreting a coefficient in simple regression and multiple regression?

Activity 3: Regression Detective

Suggested date: Thu, Oct 29
Topic: Regression Diagnostics
Time: 15–20 minutes

Scenario

Four analysts fit linear regression models.

Each model has a different possible problem.

Case A

The residual-versus-fitted plot shows a clear U-shaped pattern.

Case B

The residual spread is small for low fitted values and becomes progressively wider for high fitted values, producing a funnel shape.

Case C

The normal Q–Q plot follows the reference line in the center but bends strongly away from the line in both tails.

Case D

One observation has:

Quantity Value
Standardized residual 0.8
Leverage \(h_{ii}\) 0.41
Cook’s distance 0.92

Most other observations have leverage below 0.08 and Cook’s distance below 0.10.

Discuss

Match each case with the most likely issue:

  • nonlinearity,
  • nonconstant variance,
  • nonnormal or heavy-tailed errors,
  • unusual leverage or influence.

Then discuss:

  1. Which case suggests that the mean structure may be wrong?
  2. Which case most directly suggests nonconstant variance?
  3. Is the observation in Case D necessarily an outlier in the response direction?
  4. Should the analyst automatically delete the observation in Case D?

Group Challenge

For each case, answer:

Case What do you see? What might be wrong? What would you check or try next?
A
B
C
D
WarningMSSC 5780 Graduate Challenge

Explain the difference among:

  • an outlier,
  • a high-leverage point,
  • an influential point.

Can a point have high leverage but a small residual?

Can it still be influential?

Exit Ticket

Write one thing you would check before deleting an unusual observation.

Activity 4: Can We Trust This CI?

Suggested date: Thu, Nov 5
Topic: Bootstrap Inference
Time: 15–20 minutes

Scenario

A researcher fits a regression using a small dataset with \(n=22\).

The estimated slope is

\[ \hat{\beta}_1 = 0.66. \]

Two 95% confidence intervals are reported:

Method 95% CI for \(\beta_1\)
Usual \(t\)-interval \((-0.10,\;1.42)\)
Bootstrap percentile interval \((0.18,\;1.51)\)

The bootstrap distribution of the slope is somewhat right-skewed rather than symmetric.

Discuss

  1. Do the two intervals lead to the same conclusion about whether 0 is plausible?
  2. Why might two reasonable methods give different answers with a small sample?
  3. In plain language, what is bootstrap resampling trying to learn?
  4. Does bootstrap automatically fix:
    • confounding,
    • poor data quality,
    • extrapolation,
    • model misspecification?

Group Challenge

Consider the statement:

“The bootstrap interval is better because it does not use assumptions.”

Do you agree?

Rewrite the statement so that it is more defensible.

Then give:

  • one reason you might use bootstrap inference,
  • one reason you might still be cautious.
WarningMSSC 5780 Graduate Challenge

Compare:

  • pairs bootstrap: resample observed \((x_i,y_i)\) pairs,
  • residual bootstrap: hold the observed \(x_i\) values fixed and resample residuals.

Discuss what is treated as random in each method and what assumptions each strategy uses.

Exit Ticket

Complete this sentence:

Bootstrap can help us understand __________, but it cannot fix __________.

Activity 5: Build the Model from the Picture

Suggested date: Thu, Nov 19
Topic: Categorical Predictors and Interactions
Time: 15–20 minutes

Scenario

Two training programs are being compared.

Let

  • \(Y\) = performance score,
  • \(X\) = hours of practice,
  • \(D=0\) for Program A,
  • \(D=1\) for Program B.

The fitted lines are

\[ \text{Program A:}\qquad \widehat{Y}=50+4X, \]

and

\[ \text{Program B:}\qquad \widehat{Y}=62+1.5X. \]

Consider the interaction model

\[ Y=\beta_0+\beta_1X+\beta_2D+\beta_3XD+\epsilon. \]

Discuss

Without using software, try to determine:

  1. \(\beta_0\)
  2. \(\beta_1\)
  3. \(\beta_2\)
  4. \(\beta_3\)

Then discuss:

  1. What does \(\beta_3\) mean?
  2. Which program has the larger predicted value at \(X=0\)?
  3. Which program has the steeper relationship with practice time?
  4. At approximately what value of \(X\) do the fitted lines cross?

Group Challenge

Consider:

“Program B is better because \(\beta_2>0\).”

Why is this statement incomplete when there is an interaction?

WarningMSSC 5780 Graduate Challenge

Discuss:

  1. What hypothesis would test whether the two programs have the same slope?
  2. Why does the interpretation of \(\beta_2\) depend on what \(X=0\) means?
  3. What happens to the interpretation if we center \(X\) around 5 hours?

Exit Ticket

In one sentence:

Why can an interaction make a main effect difficult to interpret by itself?

Activity 6: Model Court

Suggested date: Thu, Dec 3
Topic: Variable Selection and Model Comparison
Time: 15–20 minutes

Scenario

Three regression models are proposed for predicting house sale price.

All performance measures use the same dataset and the same cross-validation splits.

Model Predictors Adjusted \(R^2\) AIC CV RMSE in $1,000s Maximum VIF Diagnostics
A 3 0.71 1240 31.5 2.1 Good
B 8 0.78 1208 27.9 8.7 One moderately influential point
C 15 plus interactions 0.80 1215 30.2 18.5 Several unstable coefficients

Lower AIC and lower CV RMSE are preferred.

Discuss

Suppose the primary goal is prediction of future house prices.

  1. Which model would you choose?
  2. What is the strongest evidence supporting your choice?
  3. What makes you cautious about that model?
  4. Why is choosing the model with the largest adjusted \(R^2\) alone not enough?
  5. Why is choosing the model with the smallest AIC alone not enough?

Group Challenge: Model Court

Your group is a statistical review panel.

Complete:

We recommend Model ___ because …

Use at least three pieces of information from the table.

Then identify one additional thing you would investigate before making a final decision.

WarningMSSC 5780 Graduate Challenge

Now change the goal.

Suppose the researchers want a stable and interpretable explanation using a small number of housing characteristics rather than the best possible prediction.

Would you necessarily choose the same model?

Discuss the tradeoff among:

  • prediction accuracy,
  • interpretability,
  • coefficient stability,
  • multicollinearity,
  • model complexity.

Also discuss why AIC and cross-validation may rank models differently.

Exit Ticket

Complete this sentence:

The best regression model depends on __________.
