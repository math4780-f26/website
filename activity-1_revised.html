Class Activity 1: Read a Regression

Activity 1: Read a Regression

Suggested date: Thu, Sep 17
Topic: Simple Linear Regression
Time: 15–20 minutes

Scenario

A professor studies the relationship between weekly study time and exam score.

Let

  • \(Y\) = exam score from 0 to 100,
  • \(X\) = hours studied during the week before the exam.

A simple linear regression gives

\[ \widehat{\text{Score}} = 58.4 + 3.15(\text{Study Hours}). \]

The estimated standard error of the slope is 0.62, and the 95% confidence interval for the slope is

\[ (1.91,\;4.39). \]

For a student who studies 6 hours:

  • fitted mean score: 77.3
  • 95% confidence interval for the mean score: (74.9, 79.7)
  • 95% prediction interval for one new student’s score: (66.2, 88.4)

The observed study times in the dataset range from 1 to 10 hours.

Part 1: Which Interpretation Would You Defend?

Three students interpret the slope 3.15 in different ways.

Student A

“If a student studies one additional hour, their exam score will increase by 3.15 points.”

Student B

“Students who study one additional hour tend to score about 3.15 points higher on average.”

Student C

“For every one-hour difference in study time, the model predicts a 3.15-point difference in the mean exam score.”

Discuss:

  1. Which statement would you accept as written?
  2. Which statement would you revise?
  3. Which statement would you reject?
  4. What is the statistical difference among the three statements?

Your group does not need to agree immediately. Try to defend your choice.

Part 2: What Would You Tell This Student?

A student says:

“I plan to study 6 hours. The regression says my score should be between 74.9 and 79.7.”

Discuss:

  1. What is wrong or incomplete about this statement?
  2. Which interval is more relevant for this student’s individual score?
  3. Why are the two intervals different?

Then write a one- or two-sentence response that you would actually give to the student.

Part 3: How Far Can We Trust the Line?

Another student asks:

“What score would the model predict if I study 15 hours?”

The fitted value would be

\[ 58.4 + 3.15(15) = 105.65. \]

Discuss:

  1. Should we report 105.65 as a meaningful prediction?
  2. What feature of the data should make you cautious?
  3. Is the problem mainly mathematical, statistical, or scientific?
  4. What additional information would you want before making a prediction at 15 hours?

Part 4: Does This Show That Studying Works?

Suppose the professor concludes:

“The positive slope proves that increasing study time causes higher exam scores.”

Your group must decide whether this claim is defensible.

Discuss:

  • What can the regression support?
  • What can it not establish by itself?
  • Name at least two possible variables that could be related to both study time and exam performance.

Examples might include prior preparation, motivation, course attendance, sleep, or academic background.

Then complete:

The regression provides evidence of __________, but it does not by itself establish __________.

Group Challenge

Prepare a short conclusion that answers all three questions:

  1. What does the fitted regression tell us?
  2. What prediction would you give a student who studies 6 hours?
  3. What is one conclusion you would refuse to make from this analysis?

Be prepared for any group member to explain the group’s reasoning.

WarningMSSC 5780 Graduate Challenge

Your group has concluded that the slope shows a positive association.

Now act as the statistical skeptic.

  1. Give the strongest argument for why the usual slope confidence interval might be misleading even if the calculation is numerically correct.
  2. Explain the difference between uncertainty about the mean response and uncertainty about a new observation.
  3. Suppose the residual variance increases as study time increases. Which part of the usual simple linear regression inference becomes questionable?
  4. If the scientific goal were causal rather than associational, what additional design or assumptions would be needed before interpreting the slope causally?

Your goal is not to find one “correct” answer. Your goal is to identify what assumptions support the conclusion.

Exit Ticket

Complete one of the following:

One statement from today’s activity that I would now phrase more carefully is __________.

or

One thing I would check before trusting a regression prediction is __________.
