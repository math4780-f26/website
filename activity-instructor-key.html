Class Activities — Instructor Key

ImportantInstructor-only file

Do not link this file from the public course website.

The suggested answers below are intentionally concise. The activities are designed to reward defensible statistical reasoning rather than exact wording.

Overall Facilitation Plan

For a class of 25 students:

  • Use six permanent mixed groups.
  • Each group has one MSSC 5780 student and three or four MATH 4780 students.
  • Do not announce the reporter until after discussion.
  • Ask for individual writing before discussion and an individual exit ticket afterward.
  • During reporting, ask short follow-ups such as:
    • “What evidence supports that?”
    • “What would change your answer?”
    • “What can you not conclude?”
    • “Does anyone in another group disagree?”

A useful 18-minute rhythm is:

Time Instructor action
0–3 min Silent individual attempt
3–11 min Group work; circulate and listen
11–16 min Random report from 2–3 groups/students
16–18 min Individual exit ticket

Activity 1 Key: Read a Regression

Core Answers

  1. Slope: In this sample/model, each additional hour of weekly study is associated with an estimated 3.15-point higher exam score on average.
  2. Intercept: The predicted mean score at 0 study hours is 58.4. It is mathematically part of the fitted line, but its scientific usefulness depends on whether 0 hours is within a meaningful/observed range.
  3. Prediction at 6 hours: (58.4+3.15(6)=77.3).
  4. One student’s score: Use the prediction interval, ((66.2,88.4)).
  5. Causality: The regression alone establishes an association, not a causal effect. Confounding, selection, measurement, and design matter.

Suggested Three-Sentence Group Summary

Exam score is positively associated with weekly study time; the fitted model estimates about a 3.15-point higher mean score per additional study hour. For a student studying 6 hours, the fitted score is 77.3, with a 95% prediction interval of about 66.2 to 88.4 for an individual student. Because this is an observational regression, the slope should not automatically be interpreted as the causal effect of studying.

Graduate Challenge

The mean-response CI accounts for uncertainty in estimating (E(YX=6)). The prediction interval must additionally include the irreducible person-to-person variation around that conditional mean, so it is wider.

For the usual simple-regression (t)-inference, students should mention the linear mean structure, independent errors/observations, constant error variance for the standard textbook SE, and approximate normality of errors for exact small-sample (t)-inference. The explanatory variable must also contain sufficient variation; interpretation can additionally depend on study design and measurement quality.

Exit Ticket

Expected idea:

A regression coefficient describes an estimated conditional association, but by itself it does not establish causation.

Activity 2 Key: Same (X), Different Story

Core Answers

  1. Model 1: Across students, one more study hour is associated with about 4.80 points higher final score on average.
  2. Model 2: Comparing students with the same prior GPA, one more study hour is associated with about 2.90 points higher final score on average.
  3. Prior GPA is positively associated with both study hours and final score. Model 1 partly attributes to Study Hours variation that is also related to Prior GPA.
  4. The smaller coefficient does not imply study is “less important.” The two coefficients answer different conditional questions.

Question A is closer to Model 1. Question B is directly addressed by Model 2.

“Holding Prior GPA constant” means comparing students whose prior GPA is the same (or treating prior GPA as fixed while examining how the fitted mean changes with study time).

Graduate Challenge

A useful explanation is that the simple-regression slope includes both the direct/conditional association with study time and association induced by variables omitted from the model. Once Prior GPA is included, the coefficient is a partial slope.

The coefficient does not have to decrease after adjustment. It may increase, decrease, change sign, or remain nearly unchanged depending on the covariance structure and how the omitted variable relates to both (X) and (Y).

Exit Ticket

B is preferable because it states the conditional comparison represented by the multiple-regression coefficient and avoids an automatic causal reading.

Activity 3 Key: Regression Detective

Matching

Case Likely issue Reasonable next step
A Nonlinearity / wrong mean structure Add nonlinear structure, transform variables, use polynomial/spline/local regression, or reconsider model form
B Nonconstant variance Transform response, model variance, or consider heteroskedasticity-robust inference depending on goal
C Nonnormal/heavy-tailed errors Check unusual observations; consider robust or bootstrap inference; assess whether the departure materially affects the inferential goal
D High leverage and potentially strong influence Verify the observation, inspect sensitivity with/without it, understand why its (X)-values are unusual

Case A most clearly suggests the mean structure is wrong.
Case B challenges constant variance.

Case D is not a large (Y)-direction outlier based on the standardized residual of 0.8. It has high leverage because its predictor values are unusual. Its Cook’s distance indicates that it may substantially affect fitted results.

Do not automatically delete it. Determine whether it is a data error, whether it belongs to the target population, and how conclusions change when it is included/excluded.

Graduate Challenge

  • Outlier: unusual response relative to what the model predicts; often associated with a large residual.
  • High leverage: unusual predictor configuration; large (h_{ii}).
  • Influential: materially changes fitted coefficients/predictions when included versus removed.

Yes, a point can have high leverage and a small residual. Influence depends jointly on leverage and residual size; a high-leverage point may pull the fitted model toward itself, potentially producing a modest residual while still affecting coefficients.

Exit Ticket

Expected idea:

Diagnostics help us understand how the observed data depart from the fitted model and whether those departures matter for the scientific goal.

Activity 4 Key: Can We Trust This CI?

Core Answers

  1. No. The (t)-interval includes zero; the percentile bootstrap interval does not.
  2. Small samples can make asymptotic/symmetric approximations unstable. A skewed sampling distribution can also make the two procedures differ.
  3. Bootstrap resampling uses the empirical data to approximate how an estimator would vary under repeated sampling.
  4. No. Bootstrap can quantify sampling uncertainty under a resampling scheme; it cannot repair confounding, selection bias, bad measurements, extrapolation, or an inappropriate mean structure.

A defensible rewrite of the student’s claim:

“The bootstrap can reduce reliance on a specific parametric approximation to the sampling distribution, but it still relies on assumptions about how the observed sample represents the data-generating process and on the chosen resampling scheme.”

Potential reason to prefer it: skewed or complicated estimator distribution, analytic SE difficult, desire for simulation-based uncertainty.

Reason for caution: (n=22) is small; resampling a poor or unrepresentative dataset reproduces its weaknesses.

Graduate Challenge

Pairs bootstrap: resamples observed ((X,Y)) pairs. Both predictor and response configurations vary across bootstrap samples. It is natural for random-design regression when the observed pairs are viewed as sampled from a joint population.

Residual bootstrap: holds observed (X)’s fixed, uses the fitted model, and resamples residuals to generate new responses. It more directly relies on the regression model and exchangeability/homoskedasticity of residuals in its basic form.

Exit Ticket

Expected idea:

Bootstrap helps quantify sampling variability/uncertainty; it does not fix a biased study design or a misspecified scientific question/model.

Activity 5 Key: Build the Model from the Picture

For (D=0):

[ Y=_0+_1X=50+4X, ]

so (_0=50) and (_1=4).

For (D=1):

[ Y=(_0+_2)+(_1+_3)X=62+1.5X. ]

Therefore

[ _2=12,_3=-2.5. ]

The interaction (_3=-2.5) says that Program B’s slope is 2.5 points per practice hour smaller than Program A’s slope.

Group Questions

  1. At (X=0): Program B, predicted 62 versus 50.
  2. Program A improves faster: slope 4 versus 1.5.
  3. Solve (50+4X=62+1.5X): [ 2.5X=12 X=4.8. ]
  4. (_2>0) only compares programs at (X=0). With an interaction, the program difference depends on (X): [ B-A=12-2.5X. ] Program B is predicted higher for (X<4.8), equal at 4.8, and lower for (X>4.8).

Graduate Challenge

Same slopes corresponds to

[ H_0:_3=0. ]

The meaning of (_2) is the B-versus-A difference at (X=0), so if 0 is scientifically irrelevant, the main effect may be awkward to interpret.

If (X_c=X-5), then the categorical main effect becomes the B-versus-A difference at (X=5). The fitted values and fitted surface do not change; only the parameterization/interpretation changes.

At (X=5), the difference is

[ 12-2.5(5)=-0.5. ]

Thus after centering at 5, the Program B indicator coefficient would be (-0.5).

Exit Ticket

Expected idea:

With an interaction, the effect/difference associated with one predictor depends on the value of the other predictor.

Activity 6 Key: Model Court

Prediction Goal

Model B is the strongest default choice because it has the lowest cross-validated RMSE and lowest AIC. Its adjusted (R^2) is also high.

However, students should flag:

  • VIF 8.7 suggests substantial multicollinearity,
  • there is a moderately influential point,
  • the apparent predictive advantage should be checked for stability.

Model C has the largest adjusted (R^2), but its CV RMSE is worse than Model B, AIC is worse than Model B, VIF is very high, and coefficients are unstable.

Model A is simpler and stable, but has worse predictive performance in the table.

Group Questions

  1. Adjusted (R^2) is an in-sample fit/complexity-adjusted measure and does not directly estimate future prediction error.
  2. AIC estimates an information-theoretic predictive tradeoff under a specific likelihood framework; it is useful but should not replace diagnostics or out-of-sample validation.
  3. Investigate the influential point, coefficient stability, VIF/collinearity, repeated-CV variability, and whether the predictive advantage persists under reasonable sensitivity analyses.

Graduate Challenge

For a stable, interpretable explanatory model, Model A may become preferable, depending on the substantive variables and effect-estimation goal. Model B could still be defensible if its additional predictors are scientifically important and coefficient uncertainty is acceptable.

Key tradeoff:

  • Prediction can tolerate correlated predictors if future predictions remain stable.
  • Explanation/interpretation places more weight on coefficient stability, uncertainty, scientific meaning, and parsimony.
  • AIC and CV optimize related but not identical estimates of predictive performance. AIC is likelihood-based with a complexity penalty; CV estimates held-out loss and depends on the chosen loss function/split scheme. They need not rank models identically.

Exit Ticket

Expected response:

The “best” regression model cannot be defined until we specify the goal/estimand/prediction task and criteria by which success is judged.

Suggested 30-Point Rubric

Use this rubric consistently across all six activities.

Score Individual Attempt (10) Group Reasoning (10) Exit Ticket (10)
9–10 Serious, substantially correct attempt with reasoning Actively contributes accurate reasoning; can defend/clarify group answer Correct and conceptually clear
7–8 Mostly complete; minor conceptual gap Participates meaningfully; reasoning mostly sound Mostly correct but incomplete
4–6 Partial attempt or major gap Limited contribution or relies heavily on others Shows partial understanding
1–3 Minimal attempt Minimal engagement Very weak response
0 No attempt No participation No response

For MSSC 5780, failure to make a serious attempt at the Graduate Challenge caps the Group Reasoning component at 8/10.

End-of-Semester Reflection Key

Grade primarily for evidence of thoughtful reflection rather than a predetermined answer.

Suggested 20-point rubric:

  • 8 points: specific discussion of one activity and how thinking changed;
  • 8 points: concrete example of a more careful regression conclusion;
  • 4 points: useful recurring question/habit for evaluating regression analyses.
